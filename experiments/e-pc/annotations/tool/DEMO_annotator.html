Rules that matter here

      
    
        
  • A consideration is a good at stake, a harm done or threatened, an obligation in play, a conflict between goods, or an unknown whose answer would change the moral picture. Point at the words that carry it.
    • 
        
  • A harm is a violation process that contravenes a disposition someone bears; use the six moral-foundations pairs. There is no separate harms list.
    • 
        
  • Name the bearer from the graph. It may be the person harmed, or the person whose own care disposition their act contravenes.
    • 
        
  • An unknown is a consideration when its answer would change the picture: anchor it in the sentence that says it is not known and type it by the disposition the missing answer bears on; say why in the note.
    • 
        
  • Annotate one consideration once, from the span that carries it best. Do not rank or weight.
    • 
        
  • Choose the value from the palette (one class per concept, grouped by theme, each with its definition). Tick "show every class" only when nothing in the palette fits, and say why in the note.
    • 
        
  • If no ValueNet class fits at all, do not invent one: choose the closest, set class fit to gap, describe the missing class in the note.
    • 
        
  • Do not open arms/, results/ or another annotator's file until adjudication.
    • 
      

      
Worked example (a different case, not this one)
        
A small bakery has two employees. On Monday the owner, Ada Lin, asked the newer employee, Ben Cole, to close the shop alone for the first time. The till was short by twelve pounds on Tuesday morning. Nobody has asked Ben what happened. Whether the shortfall was an error or something else is not known.

Where the export goes
        
Export writes .<yourname>.json. Email it to Aaron; he saves it under experiments/e-pc/annotations/ and runs epc.py validate-annotations. If your mail system blocks .json, choose .txt above; the content is the same. Your work is also saved in this browser as you go.
