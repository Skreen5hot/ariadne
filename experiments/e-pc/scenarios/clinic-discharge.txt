Riverbend Community Hospital is a forty-bed hospital serving a rural district. Margaret Okonjo, aged 81, was admitted to Ward 2 on the evening of 14 September 2026 after a fall in the hallway of her house. Her neighbour, Helen Adeyemi, heard the fall through the shared wall, let herself in with a spare key and called the ambulance. Margaret lives alone. The house has five concrete steps up to the front door and no handrail.

On admission Margaret completed the hospital intake form at 23:40. On it she named her son, Daniel Okonjo, as her family caregiver and signed the form herself. Daniel is 52, lives forty minutes away by car and works rotating shifts at a distribution centre. He was not present at admission. Whether Margaret had the capacity to complete the form at that hour, after the fall, is not recorded.

Dr. Priya Raman is the hospitalist responsible for Margaret's care. Tomas Weber is the case manager who coordinates discharges on Ward 2. Lena Fischer is the charge nurse. Samir Haddad is the ward physiotherapist. Robert Kaminski is the hospital's chief operating officer.

On 15 September, while Helen Adeyemi was visiting, she told Lena Fischer in the corridor that Margaret "hasn't been eating properly for weeks" and that Daniel "rarely visits". Helen asked how Margaret was doing; Lena declined to discuss Margaret's condition with her. Lena did not write the remark in the chart that day. She documented it in the nursing notes on 17 September. Nobody has asked Margaret or Daniel about what Helen said, and Helen's account has not been checked against anything else.

On the nights of 15 and 16 September, nursing staff recorded that Margaret became confused in the evening: she asked twice where her husband was (he died in 2019) and once tried to get out of bed to "make the tea". During the day she was oriented and conversational. No cognitive screen or capacity assessment has been performed. Whether the evening confusion is a delirium related to the fall and the hospital stay, which may resolve, or a sign of an underlying dementia, is not known.

On 16 September Dr. Raman started Margaret on amlodipine 5 mg daily for high blood pressure that had been recorded on admission. The patient information leaflet for amlodipine lists dizziness as a common side effect. Nobody has yet assessed whether the new medication affects Margaret's risk of falling at home.

Also on 16 September Samir Haddad carried out a physiotherapy assessment. His report states that Margaret can walk ten metres on level ground with a wheeled walking frame and can stand from a chair without help. Stairs were not assessed. The report records that she is at risk of further falls.

On 10 September, before Margaret's admission, Robert Kaminski had circulated a memo to ward staff and case managers. The memo states that the Regional Health Authority's target for average length of stay on medical wards is four days, that Riverbend is above the target, and that cases exceeding the target will be reviewed each Monday. Ward 2 was full on 17 and 18 September, and two patients were waiting in the emergency department for a bed.

On 17 September Tomas Weber telephoned Daniel Okonjo. Daniel said he could collect his mother on Friday 19 September and that he "will manage". He asked whether the hospital could arrange home help. Tomas said he would look into it. Tomas did not ask about Daniel's shift pattern, and Daniel did not volunteer it. Whether Daniel's shifts allow him to be at the house at night is not known.

Also on 17 September Margaret told Dr. Raman that she wants to go home: "I want my own bed." On 18 September, while Lena Fischer was helping her wash, Margaret said that she did not want to be "a burden on Daniel". Nobody has asked Margaret directly whether she wants Daniel to be her caregiver, or what she thinks of the plan to go home on Friday.

On 18 September Tomas Weber received an email from Comfort Care, the home care agency the hospital contracts with. The earliest date on which Comfort Care can begin visits is 9 October 2026. Tomas has not asked whether an earlier start or an interim service is possible.

The current plan, entered in the chart by Dr. Raman on 18 September, is discharge home on 19 September with Daniel as caregiver and Comfort Care visits to begin on 9 October. The plan has not yet been discussed with Margaret.

Two rules bear on the plan. Riverbend's discharge policy DP-7 requires a documented cognitive screen before discharge for any patient in whom confusion has been observed during the stay. No screen has been scheduled. Regional regulation ER-12 requires hospital staff to report a reasonable suspicion of neglect of an older person to Adult Protective Services. No one has recorded a decision about whether Helen Adeyemi's remark meets that threshold.

The following matters are not known to anyone at the hospital: whether Margaret's confusion is a delirium or a dementia; whether she had capacity when she signed the intake form; whether Daniel can be at the house at night; whether Helen's account is accurate; whether Margaret can manage the front steps with the frame; whether the amlodipine adds to her fall risk; whether Helen's remark meets the reporting threshold in ER-12; what Margaret wants regarding Daniel as her caregiver; whether Comfort Care can start earlier; and whether Daniel's "I will manage" reflects what he can do or what he feels he must say.
