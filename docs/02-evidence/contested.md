| E5 | Controlled evaluations of Analysis of Competing Hypotheses are mixed to negative: Whitesmith 2019, Dhami, Belton & Mandel 2019 (N = 50) and Maegherman et al. 2021 find no bias reduction or accuracy gain; Wilcox & Mandel 2024 review six experiments and conclude little to no benefit and possible harm; only Folker 2000 (one of two scenarios, N = 26) and Lehner et al. 2008 (novices only, N = 24) report gains. | E6 | All replications of self-distancing on wise reasoning come from the originating lab (Grossmann/Kross), two of them preregistered (N = 555); the effect was small in the largest sample (partial eta-squared = .015); distanced self-talk also increased self-interested allocation (Gainsburg et al. 2022). | E7 | Hauenstein, Thomas, Illingworth & Dougherty (2025, Psychological Science 36(1), abstract) reanalyse the Good Judgment Project data with item response theory and report that team and training effects are substantially diminished, reduced or reversed once method variance is controlled. | V01 Human flourishing | Empirical separability of hedonic and eudaimonic wellbeing is disputed: Disabato et al. 2016 (latent r = .96 across seven world regions) and Kashdan, Biswas-Diener & King 2008 (inter-measure r = .83-.87) argue for one construct; Lamers et al. 2011 (N = 1,662, Keyes co-author) find two- and three-factor models beat one factor with inter-factor r = .30-.60 | V01a, V01b |
| V04 Material wellbeing | Shape of the income-wellbeing curve above modest affluence: Kahneman & Deaton 2010 (plateau) vs Killingsworth 2021 (no plateau); Killingsworth, Kahneman & Mellers 2023 adversarial collaboration (flattening only for the least happy); Rohrer, Wenz & Arslan 2024 PNAS exchange. The low-end effect (poverty lowers wellbeing) is consistent throughout | V04 |
| V05 Experiential richness | The tripartite structure of subjective wellbeing is unresolved (Busseri & Sadava 2011: five competing models); components separable but substantially correlated. The value label itself (richness of experience) is addressed by no opened source | V05 |
| V06 Meaningful relationships | The Harvard Study cohort is small, all-male, non-representative and "was never explicitly focused on happiness" (Waldinger, 2023); "most robust predictor" is the trade book's framing, tested against competing predictors in no primary paper found | V06a |
| V07 Mastery and excellence | Meta-analytic evidence is cross-sectional self-report with significant heterogeneity; in relative-weights analyses competence relates negatively to affective commitment (Van den Broeck et al. 2016) | V07 |
| V09 Virtue / character | The six-virtue structure is not recovered in factor analyses of the VIA-IS (critiques surfaced, not opened); the strengths-wellbeing correlations are not in dispute, but kindness and integrity are among the weaker correlates (r about .19-.36) | V09, V09x |
| V11 Authentic agency | Both meta-analyses opened have very high heterogeneity (Yu et al. 2018 I² = 89%; Sutton 2020 I² = 92%); direction consistent, magnitude varies by measure and culture | V11a, V11b |
| V12 Spiritual vitality | Koenig 2012 merges spirituality into religion by declaration and so cannot separate them; Garssen, Visser & Pool 2021 (48 longitudinal studies) find r = .08 overall with only public participation and importance of religion significant; Główczyński et al. 2026 (N = 1,240) find non-religious spiritual practices associated with higher anxiety; Hood et al. 2001 report the introvertive mysticism factor predicting psychological dysfunction in US and Iranian samples | V12a, V12b |
| V14 Cultural embeddedness | Fulmer et al. 2010's person-culture match effect: Gebauer et al. 2020 (N > 3.5 million, 102 countries) find it contingent on personality, absent for some profiles, costly for others, and refer to failures to replicate it | V14x |
