# Errata to Integral Ethics v1 (ratified 2026-09-27)

`docs/01-philosophy/integral-ethics.md` is the v1 record and is never edited. The corrections
below were verified on 2026-09-26 (every URL opened is listed in `evidence-delta.md`, "v1
reference corrections") and ratified by Aaron Damiano on 2026-09-27. They are applied in
*Integral Ethics v2* where the content survives; where it does not, the erratum stands here.

| # | v1 location | v1 text | Correction | Applied |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Line 946 (in text) and line 1597 (reference list) | "depth of practice predicts benefits, not breadth (Shapiro et al., 2011)"; reference list gives Shapiro, Carlson, Astin & Freedman (2006), Mechanisms of mindfulness, J Clin Psychol 62(3), 373-386 | The 2006 reference is correct as listed (DOI 10.1002/jclp.20237) but is a theoretical model that says nothing about depth or amount of practice. A Shapiro, Brown, Thoresen & Plante (2011) paper exists (J Clin Psychol 67(3), 267-277, DOI 10.1002/jclp.20761) and is also silent on depth vs breadth. The clause "depth of practice predicts benefits, not breadth" is unsupported by either candidate at abstract level and is **removed**, not re-dated. If a source that tests practice dose is found later, it enters through a verification record | v2 does not carry the clause (v1 §5 is not carried into v2); erratum stands here |
| 2 | Line 1554 (reference list) and line 532 (in text) | Jacobsen, Buchta, Köhler & Schröger (2006), Psychological Reports, 94(4), 1253-1260; used for "aesthetic preferences show cross-cultural patterns" | Correct record: Jacobsen, T., Buchta, K., Köhler, M., & Schröger, E. (2004). The primacy of beauty in judging the aesthetics of objects. *Psychological Reports*, 94(3, Pt 2), 1253-1260. https://doi.org/10.2466/pr0.94.3c.1253-1260 (PMID 15362400). The study is a single-culture German adjective-listing task (N = 311) about the conceptual structure of "aesthetics"; it is **not cross-cultural and not about preferences** and is no longer used as evidence for that claim | v2 does not carry the claim (v1 §3 derivations are dropped); erratum stands here |
| 3 | Lines 60, 77 and 1602 | The twelve worldviews attributed to *The Riddles of Philosophy* (Steiner, 1914/2008, Anthroposophic Press) | The twelve are set out in GA 151, *Human and Cosmic Thought*, four lectures, Berlin, 20-23 January 1914: Lecture 2 introduces the twelve equally justified world-outlooks with the zodiac analogy; Lecture 3 (22 January) lists all twelve with their signs and the seven soul-moods. GA 18, *The Riddles of Philosophy* (Koelln trans., Anthroposophic Press, 1973), is a history of world conceptions whose contents carry no twelve-fold scheme. Cite: Steiner, R. (2015). *Human and Cosmic Thought* (CW 151) (C. Davy, Trans.; 3rd ed.). Rudolf Steiner Press. In text (Steiner, 1914/2015). GA 18 may be kept only as a general citation for Steiner's history of world conceptions, as the 1973 Koelln translation (the "2008" edition was not checked) | Applied in v2, "Intellectual history" |
| 4 | Line 1603 | Steiner, R. (1923/1973). The Philosophy of Freedom | The 1923 date looks doubtful (rsarchive lists the work as GA 4, first edition 1894, RSP translation 1964). Not verified further. **Flagged; no substitute date is asserted** until checked | Not carried into v2; flagged here |
