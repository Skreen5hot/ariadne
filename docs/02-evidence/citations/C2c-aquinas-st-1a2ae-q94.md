  All three loci exist and say what is attributed, with one caveat on framing.
  I-II q. 94 (six articles): a. 2 gives the first precept of natural law (good is to be done and pursued, and evil is to be avoided) and derives further precepts from the order of natural inclinations (self-preservation; inclinations shared with animals; specifically rational inclinations to know the truth and live in society). a. 4: the general principles are the same for all, but conclusions and their application can vary. a. 5: the first principles are unchangeable; secondary precepts may be added to or fail in particular cases.
  I q. 29 a. 3: "person" signifies what is most perfect in all nature, namely a subsistent individual of a rational nature; the argument turns on the high dignity (dignitas) of rational nature, which is why the name is fittingly applied to God.

fit_note: |
  Aquinas gives what the register needs in substance: an exceptionless negative precept (never kill the innocent, II-II q. 64 a. 6) that limits how goods, including the common good, may be pursued, and a grounding of the special standing of persons in the dignity of rational nature (I q. 29 a. 3). But the "side-constraint versus quantity to trade" framing is a modern (new-natural-law and post-Nozick) reading, not Aquinas's own vocabulary; he argues from the order of justice and charity, not from a constraint/goal distinction. Two further cautions: II-II q. 64 a. 2 permits public authority to kill the guilty for the common good, so the constraint Aquinas states protects the innocent rather than every person as such; and I-II q. 94 by itself says nothing about dignity or side-constraints, it supplies the natural-law framework within which q. 64's precept sits.
