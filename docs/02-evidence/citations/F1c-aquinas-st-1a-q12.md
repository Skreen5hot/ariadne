  - ST I q. 12 a. 4 co. (Corpus Thomisticum sth1003.html, paragraph tagged [28712]): "Cognitio enim contingit secundum quod cognitum est in cognoscente. Cognitum autem est in cognoscente secundum modum cognoscentis. Unde cuiuslibet cognoscentis cognitio est secundum modum suae naturae." New Advent English: "Knowledge is regulated according as the thing known is in the knower. But the thing known is in the knower according to the mode of the knower." Aquinas uses this to argue that a created intellect cannot by its natural powers see the divine essence: if the mode of being of the thing known exceeds the mode of the knower's nature, knowledge of it is above that knower's nature.
  - ST I q. 75 a. 5 co. (sth1075.html): "omne quod recipitur in aliquo, recipitur in eo per modum recipientis"; and the intellectual soul "cognoscit rem aliquam in sua natura absolute, puta lapidem inquantum est lapis absolute". New Advent: "whatever is received into something is received according to the condition of the recipient"; "the intellectual soul knows a thing in its nature absolutely: for instance, it knows a stone absolutely as a stone." Used to argue the soul is not composed of matter and form.
  - ST I q. 84 a. 1 co. (sth1084.html): the intellect receives the species of material, mobile bodies "immaterialiter et immobiliter, secundum modum suum, nam receptum est in recipiente per modum recipientis". New Advent: "the received is in the receiver according to the mode of the receiver."

  Status of the maxim: in none of the loci opened does the sentence "quidquid recipitur per (ad) modum recipientis recipitur" occur verbatim. Aquinas states the principle in variant forms ("omne quod recipitur in aliquo, recipitur in eo per modum recipientis"; "receptum est in recipiente per modum recipientis"; "cognitum est in cognoscente secundum modum cognoscentis"). The New Catholic Encyclopedia entry "Scholastic Terms and Axioms" (R. Smith; via encyclopedia.com) lists "Quidquid recipitur ad modum recipientis recipitur" as a scholastic axiom, glossed "Whatever is received is received after the mode of the one receiving", without assigning it a specific Aquinas locus. The Lonergan Institute page describes Aquinas as repeatedly "referring to" the principle and cites ST 1a q.75 a.5; 3a q.5; 1a q.12 a.4; q.14 a.1 ad 3; q.16 a.1; q.19 a.6 ad 2; SCG 2.79; De Veritate q.2 a.3 (only the four ST I loci above were opened). Conclusion: the Latin tag is a scholastic formula distilled from Aquinas's repeated statements, not a verbatim quotation; the canonical form uses "ad modum", while Aquinas's own phrasing in the loci opened is "per modum recipientis" / "secundum modum cognoscentis".

fit_note: |
  The attribution is accurate: Aquinas holds that what is known exists in the knower according to the knower's mode, so knowledge is relative to the nature of the knower while the thing known is one and unchanged (the stone is known "as a stone", the divine essence exceeds the created mode). That is the epistemic core of perspectival realism -- aspect-relativity of knowing, unity of the object -- and the register reads it correctly.
  Only partial support for the EXACT claim, for two reasons. (1) Nothing in these loci concerns morality or "morally relevant features". (2) Aquinas's "mode of the knower" contrasts kinds of knowers (sense vs. intellect, created vs. divine), not a plurality of equally legitimate human standpoints on the same object; the step from "each knower knows according to its mode" to "no single perspective captures all features" is the register's own inference, and Aquinas would hold that the divine intellect does capture all.
