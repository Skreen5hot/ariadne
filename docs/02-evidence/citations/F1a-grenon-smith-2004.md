  What it says, by location (section names from the article; page refs are the journal pagination 69-103 where the PDF shows it, otherwise the running-head numbers 138-170 printed on the author-hosted copy):
  - Abstract (p. 69): proposes a modular ontology combining a purely spatial ontology of snapshot views at instants (SNAP) with a purely spatiotemporal ontology of change and process (SPAN); dynamic spatial ontology "must combine these two distinct types of inventory of the entities and relationships in reality."
  - Introduction (pp. 69-70): the snapshot view and the process view "have traditionally been perceived as incompatible"; the theory is "designed to do justice to what is of value in both"; the approach is "a joint venture between the so-called three-dimensionalist and four-dimensionalist perspectives."
  - Section 1 Philosophical Background, methodology paragraph (running-head pp. 138-139): the methodology is "realist, perspectivalist, fallibilist, and adequatist". Perspectivalism: "there may be alternative, equally legitimate perspectives on reality", constrained by realism (not every view is legitimate; views must survive critical tests). Adequatism is defined against reductionism ("among the plurality of alternative views of reality there is some one, basic view to which all the others ought to be reduced"); the authors instead "affirm that there are many views of reality which are equally veridical" -- views of different domains, from different perspectives, or at different levels of granularity -- and state that a plurality of such views is needed to do justice to reality as a whole.
  - Section 1, "Temporal Modes of Being" (running-head pp. 140-141): SNAP is the ontology of continuants, SPAN of occurrents; the framework combines "in the spirit of adequatism" a three-dimensionalist and a four-dimensionalist perspective, "positions which are normally seen as representing mutually incompatible views of reality." A reductionist four-dimensionalist would keep only SPAN, a reductionist three-dimensionalist only SNAP; the authors "insist that, if we want to do justice to the whole of reality in non-reductionistic fashion, then we need both types of component."
  - Section 4.1 Processual Entities (running-head p. 154): "Processes are the entities revealed when we adopt a certain special (SPAN) perspective on reality. But the orthogonal (SNAP) perspective is no less faithful to what exists."
  - Section 5 Trans-Ontology in BFO, "Qualitative Change" (running-head p. 158): contradictory properties may be ascribed to one object provided the ascriptions belong to distinct ontologies "such that there is no single perspective within which they conjunctively obtain."
  - Section 6 Case Study (running-head p. 161): a geographical ontology "can thus be limited neither to objects nor to processes, but must make room for both."
  The attributed content (SNAP and SPAN as complementary, partial, mutually non-reducible views of one reality) is therefore confirmed and stated explicitly in Sections 1, 4.1 and 5.
