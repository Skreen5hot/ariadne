  The book is constructive: it shows how to aggregate plural criteria into one ranking (a multiattribute
  utility function) given independence conditions and elicited scaling constants. It does not argue
  that no neutral aggregation exists. What it does support is the neighbouring claim: "aggregating
  plural criteria into one ranking is possible only by supplying decision-maker-specific tradeoff
  judgments (scaling constants) and independence assumptions; the aggregation is therefore value-laden,
  not neutral". If the register's "neutral" means "value-free", that neighbouring claim is what is
  wanted and this book is consistent with it; if the claim means impossibility (the Arrow reading),
  Keeney & Raiffa is a counterexample under cardinal, comparable utilities. Better-fitting source for
  the impossibility reading: Arrow & Raynaud (1986), Social Choice and Multicriterion Decision-Making,
  MIT Press (recorded under C5a).
