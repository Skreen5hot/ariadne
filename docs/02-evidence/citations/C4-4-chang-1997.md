  Content of Chang 2002 (pp. 659-662 read directly): hard cases of comparison (Mozart vs Michelangelo; accounting vs skydiving; a duty to keep promises vs a requirement to avoid causing unnecessary pain) are ones where it seems neither item is better and they are not equally good. Chang argues against the Trichotomy Thesis (that better, worse and equally good exhaust the positive comparative relations) and proposes a fourth relation, "on a par": if A is neither better nor worse than B and they are not equally good, they may nevertheless be comparable. Parity is offered precisely so that hard conflicts need not be treated as incomparability, ignorance, or vagueness; each item retains its value and the comparison is a real one.
