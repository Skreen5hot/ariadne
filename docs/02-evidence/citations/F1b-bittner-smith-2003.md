  - Section 5.1 Recognizing mereological structure: "To impose a partition on a given domain of reality is to foreground certain objects and features in that domain and trace over others"; partitions are granular "in virtue precisely of the fact that a partition can recognize an object without recognizing all its parts."
  - Section 5.2-5.3 (granularity): a three-cell partition animals/dogs/cats "represents its domain correctly; it just falls far short of a certain desirable completeness. Partitions (the one-cell "Spinoza partition" aside) "are partial in their focus. In foregrounding some regions of reality each partition thereby traces over everything that lies outside its domain." Tracing over of parts is "manifested by every partition" unless mereological atomism is true.

## Notes
- Identification of the work: the citation as given ("Bittner & Smith, granular partitions") could refer to several papers. Per Bittner's publications page (opened) the family includes: "A Theory of Granular Partitions" (2003, Foundations of GIScience, Taylor & Francis, pp. 117-151; PDF link http://www.acsu.buffalo.edu/~bittner3/tgp.pdf) and "A Taxonomy of Granular Partitions" (2001, COSIT 2001, LNCS, Springer, pp. 28-43). Search results also list "Granular Partitions and Vagueness" (2001, FOIS) and Bittner, Smith and Donnelly, "The logic of systems of granular partitions"; none of these three was opened. The 2003 chapter is the fullest statement and is the one that carries the attribution (transparency vs. completeness/exhaustiveness; partiality; foregrounding and tracing over), so it is the one resolved here.
- Access method: the PDF at the acsu.buffalo.edu URL was fetched with WebFetch (binary PDF returned and saved locally), then text-extracted with pdftotext and read for the sections cited. The ontology.buffalo.edu copy was downloaded with curl (WebFetch rejected that host's certificate) and has identical text; its header supplies the printed book citation and page range.
- Chapter number discrepancy: the two author-hosted PDFs say "Chapter 7" and "Chapter 1" respectively; a search-result snippet for PhilPapers says "Chapter 6". I did not verify the chapter number against the printed book.
