fit: partially_supports
fit_note: |
  "Stable structure": strongly supported. The circular ordering of ten value types on two bipolar
  dimensions replicates across 20 countries (1992), 88 samples in 40 countries (1995), 71 representative
  ESS samples in 32 countries (2011), and a refined 19-value model in 10 countries (2012).
  "Mutual conflict": supported under the theory's interpretation, with a caveat. The evidence is
  the correlational geometry of self-reported importance ratings: opposing value types sit on opposite
  sides of the circle (priorities negatively related), adjacent ones are compatible. Schwartz reads
  the bipolar oppositions as motivational conflict, and the abstract asserts consistent value
  conflicts. But the data show opposition of priorities across people, not directly that pursuing
  one value frustrates another within a person. The claim fits if "conflict" means structural
  incompatibility of priorities; it over-reads the evidence if it means experienced or behavioural
  conflict.
