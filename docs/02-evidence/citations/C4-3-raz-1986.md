  Content, from the SEP entry "Incommensurable Values" (Andersson and Hsieh, rev. 26 June 2025), which cites Raz by page: Raz defines two options as incommensurable when neither is better than the other and they are not of equal value, so that even an ordinal ranking is impossible; his example is the choice between a career in law and one as a clarinettist, where neither seems better and they do not seem equally good either (Raz 1986, p. 332); and he argues that some incommensurabilities are constitutive of goods, e.g. the judgment that companionship is incommensurable with money (p. 350). On this account the incommensurable options are each genuinely valuable; the point is that they cannot be ranked, not that one lacks value.
