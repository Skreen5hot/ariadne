  Content confirmed: Ross introduces "prima facie duty" (or "conditional duty") for the characteristic an act has, in virtue of being of a certain kind, of being a duty proper if it were not also of another morally significant kind. He lists, without claiming completeness, duties of fidelity, reparation, gratitude, justice, beneficence, self-improvement and non-maleficence. When more than one prima facie duty is incumbent, one must judge which is more incumbent in the circumstances; that one becomes the duty proper. Crucially for the claim: a prima facie duty is not a mere appearance that may turn out illusory but "an objective fact involved in the nature of the situation"; and when we are justified in breaking a promise to relieve distress "we do not for a moment cease to recognize a prima facie duty to keep our promise". The act tends to be wrong qua promise-breaking and tends to be right qua relieving distress at the same time.
