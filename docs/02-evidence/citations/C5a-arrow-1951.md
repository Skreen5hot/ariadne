fit_note: |
  Arrow's theorem is about aggregating several individuals' ordinal preference orderings into a social
  ordering. The register claim is about aggregating several evaluative criteria within one decision.
  The transfer is an analogy (criteria in place of persons), acknowledged in the literature and made
  explicit by Arrow & Raynaud (1986). Under the analogy the theorem says: no rule using only the
  ordinal rankings by each criterion can satisfy unrestricted domain, Pareto, independence and
  non-dictatorship at once. That is narrower than "no neutral aggregation": (a) "neutral" must be
  read as "satisfying Arrow's conditions"; (b) the theorem does not show aggregation is impossible,
  only that any rule violates one condition (a lexical or dictatorial priority among criteria, or a
  cardinal weighted sum as in Keeney & Raiffa, aggregates fine); (c) the result depends on using
  ordinal, non-comparable information only. So it supports the claim for ordinal, tradeoff-free
  aggregation and by analogy.

## Notes
If the register keeps the multi-criteria reading, Arrow & Raynaud (1986) is the citation that makes
the analogy explicit and should sit alongside Arrow (1951). Note that Arrow's own conditions do not
include a condition called "neutrality" (that belongs to May's theorem); the claim's word "neutral"
should be glossed.
